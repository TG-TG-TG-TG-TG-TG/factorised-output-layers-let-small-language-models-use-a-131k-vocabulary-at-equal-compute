In small language models the output layer can take most of the training compute: at d=384 with a 131k vocabulary a flat softmax head is 79.5% of training FLOPs. We compare output layers at equal training FLOPs, in bits per byte (BPB): a flat head, an adaptive softmax, and a two-level head from the class-based family (8,192 frequent tokens with their own logits, the other tokens in 4,096 k-means clusters with a within-cluster softmax; the cost of building the clusters is charged). Both factorised heads beat the flat head. With either of them, the 131k model minus the best 50k model of our main comparison is −0.0095 / −0.0092 BPB (two-level / adaptive softmax); with the flat head, 131k minus 50k is +0.0495. The usual reduced-width tail of adaptive softmax hurts here: scoring the tail at full width changes its BPB by −0.0107 / −0.0093 (seeds 0 / 1), and it then ties the two-level head (two-level minus adaptive softmax −0.0010 / +0.0004, a tie under a rule fixed before the run), with 108.1M parameters (its tail is untied) against 62.5M. Against the flat head, the two-level head trains at 1.98× (50k) and 4.29× (131k) the tokens per second on our GPU, is +0.0115 BPB worse at equal tokens, and its equal-compute advantage at 50k roughly halves per doubling of compute (0.5 to 2× the budget; seed 0). The 131k BPE is trained on the training corpus and the 50k tokenizer is GPT-2's, so vocabulary size and domain change together. The compared models have 6 blocks and d=384, train on English FineWeb-Edu with one learning rate, and use 1–2 seeds. Code, logs and results: https://github.com/TG-TG-TG-TG-TG-TG/factorised-output-layers-let-small-language-models-use-a-131k-vocabulary-at-equal-compute.

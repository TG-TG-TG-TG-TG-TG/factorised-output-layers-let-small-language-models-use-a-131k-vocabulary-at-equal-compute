# Round 11 notes

The numbers are in `round11_summary.md` and `round11_results.json`, generated by `PYTHONIOENCODING=utf8 python round11_analyze.py`.

## One-line answer

**Is the two-level head cheap AND as good as the flat head?**

- **Cheap and better per FLOP:** yes. At equal training FLOPs, H3\*-384 beats the flat head B384 by −0.023 BPB on FineWeb-Edu and by −0.025 to −0.031 out of domain. It beats an 8k-vocabulary flat head by −0.032 BPB. There is no BLiMP or knowledge (E4) penalty, and decode is 0.72× the flat head's ms/token.
- **As good at equal tokens:** no. It is still ~0.03 nats behind, all of it in tokens ranked ≥ 4,096. Exact re-scoring of the top-16 tail clusters doesn't close the gap: −0.005 nats vs H3k8c4, mean of two seeds.

## Where the brief's conditions sit

- **E4 exposures (the Capo note):** E4 runs at 100 exposures per biography, as the note asks (99.99–100.0 in `eval_bios.jsonl`). The one exception is the equal-FLOPs H3k8c4 arm, which the brief asks for explicitly: 237.4 exposures, which is 100 × FLOPs/token(B) / FLOPs/token(H3k8c4).
  - The models are not at their capacity ceiling. Bits stored still grow from N = 20k to N = 100k (B: 387k → 712k), and the non-embedding bits/param are 0.25–0.71, below Capo's ~1 bit/param at 100 exposures.
  - So E4 measures how much each head stores for a fixed population and budget. It does not measure a bits-per-parameter capacity limit.
  - Measuring the limit would need N large enough that bits stop growing, e.g. 300k–1M at 100 exposures. *(Post-hoc observation; not a pinned rule.)*
- **Seed rule (pinned):**
  - Part C (−0.0039 vs H3k8c4, within 0.010 of the 0.010 "partial" threshold) ran tH3rs16 seed 1. H3k8c4 seed 1 already existed from round 10.
  - E2 (BLiMP Δs +0.19 / +0.38 / −0.29 / −0.21, within ±1 point of the 1-point threshold) ran seed 1 for B384, H3\*-384 (300M branch and 575M) and B_r8. H3k8c4 and tH3rs16 seed 1 checkpoints already existed.
  - No other verdict was inside its band:
    - Part B: −0.0315 vs ±0.003.
    - E1: −0.031 / −0.025 vs a −0.003 threshold.
    - E4: 1.105 / 1.015 vs 0.95 ± 0.05.
    - Part D: no threshold; |Δ| 0.013 > 0.010.
- **2-hour rule:** every smoke estimate was under 2 h, so no horizon was cut. The longest was tH3rs64 at 117 min, and it didn't run.
- **Budget:** 7.65 GPU-h for the main round, plus ~2.5 h for the E2 seed-1 runs. The total stays under the 10 h ceiling.

## Surprises

1. **Small vocabularies do much better on LAMBADA.**
   - Accuracy: F8k-384 16.8% and F4k-384 17.3%, against 14.2% for H3\*-384 and 14.8% for B384.
   - Word bits/byte: 1.40 / 1.32 against 1.70 / 1.71.
   - This holds even though both small-vocabulary models are worse on FineWeb BPB. The gain is concentrated on words whose first GPT-2 token is rare, and F8k is also slightly better on PG-19 (−0.007 BPB vs H3\*-384), which is also book text.
   - I checked the evaluator: context and target are encoded separately, and GPT-2 pre-tokenisation splits before " word" in all tokenizers, so the scoring is the same for every model. I found no bug.
   - A plausible reading is that copying or composing a rare name piece by piece is easier than predicting it as one rare token.
2. **Re-scoring helps the mid-tail and hurts the rarest tokens.**
   - At d=128, tH3rs16 improves [4096, 16384) (+0.148 vs +0.168 for H3k8c4).
   - It worsens [16384, V) (+0.319 vs +0.250) and in-context copying of rare tokens (E5b copy-2 Δ +0.34 vs −0.84).
   - The correct cluster is among the 16 re-scored ones for only 43% of tail targets (36% in the rarest bucket).
3. **At d=384, re-scoring helps more than at d=128, at a wall-clock cost.** H3rs16-384 at 575M reaches 3.7027 vs 3.7120 for H3\*-384, −0.009 nats at about the same FLOPs (0.550× vs 0.548× B). But it is ~2.1× slower per step (75 vs 35 min estimated for the run).
4. **At equal FLOPs, the two-level head copies random rare-token lists much better than the flat head.** E5b copy-2: 7.54 vs 9.01 nats. At equal tokens it is worse (+0.30).
5. **Decode at d=128 shows no speed-up.** Batch-1 decode at d=128 is 0.143 ms/token for both flat and two-level heads, because the head isn't the bottleneck at that size. At d=384 the two-level head is 0.72× the flat head.
6. **Part D:** H3\*-512 at matched FLOPs (≈395M tokens) reaches 3.6989, −0.013 nats vs H3\*-384 575M. That is in favour of going wider, but on one seed only.

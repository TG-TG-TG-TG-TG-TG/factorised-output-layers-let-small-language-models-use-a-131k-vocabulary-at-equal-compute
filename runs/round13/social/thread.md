I thought my two-level head was what let a tiny LM use a 131k vocab. Then I gave adaptive softmax a full-width tail, and it tied (-0.0010 / +0.0004 bits/byte, 2 seeds). What holds: at equal training FLOPs, factorised heads beat a flat one. Paper: [LINK] 🧵

2/8 [244 chars] — images: runs/round13/social/schematic_1600x900.png

Method: 6-block GPT, d=384, FineWeb-Edu. Every arm gets the same training FLOPs, including the cost of building the clusters, and is scored in bits per byte on the same validation text. Decision rules were written down before the deciding runs.

3/8 [240 chars] — images: runs/round13/social/headcost_1600x900.png

Vocabulary, 50k → 131k at equal FLOPs: the flat head gets worse by 0.049 bits/byte (it spends 80% of its FLOPs on the head). The 131k two-level and full-width adaptive-softmax models are 0.010 and 0.009 better than the best d=384 50k model.

4/8 [268 chars]

Adaptive softmax usually scores rare tokens through narrow projections (96 and 24 dims here). Making that tail full width changed it by -0.011 / -0.009 bits/byte: from behind the two-level head to a tie. Params: 62.5M two-level vs 108.1M adaptive (its tail is untied).

5/8 [193 chars]

At a 50k vocab the two-level head ties a (narrow-tail) adaptive softmax: -0.001 bits/byte (2 seeds). Both beat the flat head (-0.014 and -0.013), so the gain comes from factorising the softmax.

6/8 [217 chars] — images: runs/round13/social/compute_1600x900.png

The gain over the flat head shrinks with compute: -0.023, -0.013, -0.006 bits/byte at 0.5×, 1×, 2× the budget (50k, seed 0). Larger budgets are untested. Per token the two-level head is worse (+0.012 at equal tokens).

7/8 [219 chars] — images: runs/round13/social/speed_1600x900.png, runs/round13/social/memory_1600x900.png

Why it works: at d=384 the flat head is 60% of training FLOPs. The two-level head costs 0.245× as much, so the same budget buys more tokens. On our GPU its training step runs at 2.0× the tokens/s of flat (4.3× at 131k).

8/8 [237 chars]

Caveats: tiny models (≤109M params), 1–2 seeds, one English dataset, one LR, untuned adaptive softmax (untied tail). The 131k tokenizer is in-domain, the 50k one is GPT-2's. Old idea: Goodman 2001, Morin & Bengio 2005, Grave et al. 2017.
